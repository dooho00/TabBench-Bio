Living Benchmark

How TabBench-Bio Evolved

24 November 2026 Coverage: 21 → 39 evaluated models across 46 datasets

TabBench-Bio was set up as a living benchmark because tabular foundation models (TFMs) have been shipping faster than static papers can evaluate them. In the two months following the initial release, the roster grew from 21 evaluated models in the submitted paper to 39 configurations today (37 configured peer models plus Constant and AutoGluon).

Each new batch ran on the same frozen five-fold splits, the same 28 feature-by-sample cells, and the same strict failure policy. Keeping the rules fixed makes it possible to trace exactly how much the top of the leaderboard moved as new models arrived.

Nine of the current top ten arrived after the paper

The initial paper snapshot ranked RealTabPFN 2.5 first at the reference cell (Elo 1,156 in the 42-target paper snapshot; 1,159 on today's 44-target pool), followed by TabPFN 3 and Logistic Regression. Two months later, nine of the top ten evaluated configurations at p = 10,000, n = 100 were added after that baseline release, pushing RealTabPFN 2.5 to ninth overall (seventh among default-visible models).

Notice how quickly the top group compressed as well. Kumo Tabular (L) (1,241; 95% CI 1,183–1,302), Lattice (1,231; 1,177–1,288), Kumo Tabular (M) (1,225; 1,159–1,288), Seldon (1,212; 1,149–1,278), and FTFM-1 ‡ (1,210; 1,154–1,268) all sit within roughly 30 Elo points of each other, with overlapping target-bootstrap intervals. We will continue adding new models to the benchmark under the same frozen splits and protocol as they are released.

You can inspect every model's behaviour across individual modalities, feature budgets, and failure rates in the model registry, or review individual updates in the changelog.
